# DropZone Chauffeur — Business Model & Unit Economics (Starter)

## 1. Purpose
This document defines a starter business model for DropZone Chauffeur so the product, pricing, and operations roadmap can align to a realistic commercial approach.

## 2. Recommended MVP Business Model
For MVP, DropZone should launch as a **managed premium chauffeur marketplace** in one UAE city corridor.

### Recommended launch model
- **Primary model:** B2C premium airport + business transport booking platform
- **Secondary model:** B2B corporate accounts for repeat airport/business trips
- **Supply model:** Licensed chauffeur companies / fleet partners fulfill rides
- **Customer promise:** premium, scheduled, reliable, trackable, English/Arabic, digital payment enabled

## 3. Recommended MVP Launch Scope
### Geography
- Start with **Dubai** only
- Focus corridor:
  - Dubai International Airport (DXB)
  - Business districts
  - Key hotels / corporate zones

### Trip types
- Airport pickup
- Airport drop-off
- Scheduled business transfer

### Excluded from MVP
- Full on-demand ride hailing
- Inter-emirate expansion
- Corporate bulk fleet management
- Hourly / multi-stop if they delay stable launch

## 4. Target Customer Segments
### Segment A — Premium individual travelers
- Business travelers
- Hotel guests
- Frequent flyers
- High-income residents needing scheduled premium rides

### Segment B — Corporate accounts
- SMEs and executive offices
- Travel desks / office admins
- Companies requiring airport transfers for staff or guests

## 5. Value Proposition
### For passengers
- Premium scheduled chauffeur experience
- Better reliability than WhatsApp/manual coordination
- Real-time tracking, clear booking records, digital payment
- English/Arabic localized experience

### For corporate clients
- Centralized bookings
- Invoice-ready trip records
- Better traceability than fragmented driver calls/messages

### For fleet partners
- More ride volume
- Dispatch visibility
- Cleaner digital workflow
- Potential recurring demand from airport/business routes

## 6. Revenue Model
## Option selected for MVP
Use a **commission-per-completed-booking** model first.

### Recommended commercial structure
- Platform take rate: **18%** starter assumption
- Partner/fleet receives: **82%** of net booking value before platform-level adjustments
- Card/payment processing fees treated as platform cost unless contract says otherwise

### Future revenue add-ons
- Corporate monthly account fee
- Priority dispatch / SLA fee
- Admin dashboard subscription for partner fleets
- Concierge / meet-and-greet add-ons
- Hourly booking premium

## 7. Pricing Model (Starter)
Pricing should be transparent and rules-based.

### Recommended fare components
- Base fare by vehicle class
- Airport service fee
- Distance/time estimate component
- Night / peak adjustment (later)
- Meet & greet add-on (later)
- Waiting time / no-show fee (policy dependent)

### Vehicle classes
- Business Sedan
- Premium SUV
- Executive Van (later if supply supports it)

## 8. Unit Economics — Starter Assumptions
These are placeholder assumptions for internal planning only.

### Example assumptions
- Average booking value (AOV): AED 220
- Platform take rate: 18%
- Gross revenue per booking: AED 39.6
- Estimated payment/ops/support cost per booking: AED 12–18
- Contribution margin target per booking after variable costs: positive by pilot stabilization

### What must be validated
- Real market AOV by corridor
- Partner payout expectations
- Customer willingness to pay vs hotel limo/Careem premium alternatives
- Support cost per booking
- Cancellation/refund cost leakage

## 9. Corporate Model (Post-MVP)
Corporate should start as a controlled extension, not as a full second product during MVP.

### Recommended early corporate offer
- Company account
- Centralized invoicing
- Named travelers / guests
- Monthly billing or pre-funded balance
- Manual approval workflow only if demanded by pilot customers

## 10. KPIs
### Commercial
- Bookings per week
- Completed trip rate
- Average booking value
- Platform revenue
- Repeat booking rate

### Operational
- Driver acceptance rate
- On-time pickup rate
- Cancellation rate
- Average assignment time
- Support tickets per 100 bookings

### Customer trust
- Passenger rating
- Complaint rate
- Refund rate
- NPS / satisfaction (later)

## 11. Business Risks
- Regulatory approvals may delay launch
- Supply-side quality inconsistency may damage brand
- Premium positioning may fail without strong service standards
- Stripe/payment availability in UAE may require gateway change
- Corporate scope may distract from B2C MVP stability

## 12. Immediate Decisions Required
1. Confirm launch city and corridor
2. Confirm whether supply is partner fleets or owned operations
3. Confirm take rate target
4. Confirm payment provider viability in UAE
5. Confirm whether corporate is MVP or post-MVP

## 13. Recommendation
For starter execution, DropZone should be positioned as:
> A premium scheduled chauffeur marketplace for Dubai airport and business transport, fulfilled by verified licensed fleet partners, monetized primarily through commission on completed trips.
