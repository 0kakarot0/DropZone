# DropZone Chauffeur — Driver Supply & Onboarding Operating Model (Starter)

## 1. Purpose
This document defines a starter operating model for driver supply, partner onboarding, and service quality.

## 2. Recommended MVP Supply Strategy
For MVP, avoid recruiting independent drivers directly.
Start with **licensed chauffeur / limousine fleet partners**.

### Why this model is better for MVP
- Faster launch
- Lower regulatory complexity
- Existing vehicle supply
- Easier replacement if a driver cancels
- Better service control through partner contracts

## 3. Supply Model Options
### Recommended now
- **Partner fleet model**
  - DropZone acquires and manages demand
  - Licensed fleet partners provide chauffeurs and vehicles
  - Platform handles booking, assignment visibility, and customer experience

### Future options
- Hybrid model with direct drivers
- Owned premium fleet
- White-label fleet SaaS

## 4. Partner Profile for MVP
Ideal partner fleets should have:
- Licensed commercial/chauffeur operations
- Premium sedan/SUV inventory
- Reliable airport transfer experience
- English-speaking drivers
- Ability to accept digital assignment workflow
- Willingness to meet service standards and SLAs

## 5. Driver Quality Standard
Every active driver assigned through the platform should meet minimum quality standards:
- Verified identity and documentation
- Professional appearance and behavior
- Vehicle cleanliness
- Punctuality and route discipline
- Basic English communication
- Use of smartphone and app workflows
- Willingness to comply with support/escalation processes

## 6. Onboarding Flow
### Stage 1 — Partner qualification
- Business profile received
- Service areas reviewed
- Fleet size reviewed
- Vehicle classes reviewed
- Operating licenses reviewed
- Commercial terms discussed

### Stage 2 — Contracting
- Commercial agreement signed
- Service standards / SLA accepted
- Cancellation/no-show rules accepted
- Payment and payout rules accepted
- Data/privacy obligations accepted

### Stage 3 — Driver onboarding
- Driver roster submitted
- Driver documents verified
- Vehicle details mapped
- Driver app/account created
- Training completed

### Stage 4 — Activation
- Test ride / dry run passed
- Support contact assigned
- Partner moved to active dispatch pool

## 7. Dispatch Operating Rules
Starter rules:
- Only available and verified drivers can receive assignments
- One active ride per driver at a time
- Airport rides prioritized for trained airport-capable drivers
- If a driver rejects or times out, booking moves to next eligible driver
- If booking remains unassigned beyond threshold, ops escalation is triggered

## 8. Service-Level Expectations
Suggested starter SLAs:
- Driver response to assignment: within defined minutes
- Driver arrival punctuality target
- Cleanliness and dress-code compliance
- Passenger communication standard
- Incident reporting within the same shift/day

## 9. Performance Management
Track per driver and per fleet:
- Acceptance rate
- Cancellation after acceptance
- On-time pickup rate
- Passenger ratings
- Complaint rate
- Completion rate

## 10. Suspension / Deactivation Triggers
A driver or partner may be suspended for:
- Expired or invalid documentation
- Unsafe conduct
- Repeated no-shows
- Severe passenger complaints
- Fraudulent trip behavior
- Repeated SLA breaches

## 11. Incentives
Starter incentives can include:
- Higher priority for high-performing fleets
- Lower dispute friction for reliable partners
- Premium booking allocation
- Fast payout cycles for top performers

## 12. Risks
- Cold-start supply if no partner signs
- Inconsistent service quality across fleets
- Last-minute driver cancellations
- Poor airport execution damaging premium brand
- Driver resistance to app workflow or location tracking

## 13. Immediate Next Steps
1. Create partner qualification checklist
2. Draft partner commercial agreement
3. Draft driver document checklist
4. Create driver training SOP
5. Define SLA metrics and thresholds
6. Define ops escalation path for unassigned rides

## 14. Recommendation
Use a partner-fleet operating model first, prove demand and service quality, then evaluate whether direct-driver expansion is justified.
