# DropZone Chauffeur — UAE Compliance & Operating Assumptions (Starter)

## 1. Purpose
This document captures starter compliance and operating assumptions for planning. It is **not legal advice** and must be reviewed with UAE legal/regulatory counsel before launch.

## 2. Why This Document Exists
The product operates in transport, payments, identity verification, and location tracking. These areas create legal and operational obligations that must be acknowledged early.

## 3. Core Assumptions
- DropZone will only launch in a jurisdiction where the operating model is legally permitted.
- Drivers/fleet partners must hold valid local licenses/permits required for chauffeur/limo transport.
- User identity, trip, payment, and location data will be handled under applicable UAE privacy and commercial rules.
- The company will not represent itself as licensed for regulated transport unless that status is confirmed.

## 4. Transport / Fleet Assumptions
Before launch, confirm:
- Whether DropZone acts as:
  - marketplace only,
  - dispatcher/aggregator,
  - transportation operator,
  - or software provider for licensed fleets
- Which permits or commercial approvals are required
- Whether each partner fleet must maintain its own regulatory compliance and insurance
- Whether airport pickup rules require special permits or coordination

## 5. Driver / Vehicle Documentation Assumptions
Each active driver should have verified:
- Government-issued identity
- Driver license
- Chauffeur / commercial transport eligibility if required
- Employment or partner-fleet affiliation proof
- Vehicle registration
- Insurance validity
- Vehicle category/class compatibility with promised service tier

## 6. Payment Assumptions
- Online payments must use a legally supported payment provider for the operating entity.
- Refund handling, chargebacks, and cancellation fees must be clearly documented.
- Invoices/receipts must meet business and tax record needs.
- Stored payment methods must rely on PCI-compliant third-party providers rather than in-house storage.

## 7. Privacy / Data Assumptions
The system handles:
- Identity data
- Contact details
- Payment-linked metadata
- Live location data
- Trip history
- Driver operational data

Starter assumptions:
- Collect only necessary data
- Obtain user consent where required
- Document retention periods
- Support account deletion/deactivation workflow
- Restrict role-based access to user and driver data
- Avoid exposing driver private details beyond operational necessity

## 8. Trust & Safety Assumptions
The platform should define:
- Emergency / escalation contact path
- Complaint handling ownership
- Driver suspension triggers
- Fraud / abuse review process
- Passenger no-show and abuse handling
- Lost item handling responsibility

## 9. Operating Policies Required Before Launch
The following policy documents should exist before production launch:
- Terms of service
- Privacy policy
- Cancellation and refund policy
- Driver/fleet partner agreement
- Incident escalation process
- Support SLA guide

## 10. Open Compliance Questions
1. What exact licenses are required for Dubai launch?
2. Can DropZone legally act as a booking marketplace without operating vehicles itself?
3. Is Stripe viable for the target legal entity and geography?
4. What are the invoicing/VAT obligations?
5. What location-data retention and deletion rules apply?
6. What airport pickup-specific restrictions apply at launch?

## 11. Recommended Pre-Launch Checklist
- Legal review completed
- Operating entity confirmed
- Payment gateway validated
- Fleet partner agreement template created
- Driver verification checklist finalized
- Privacy/terms/refund policies drafted
- Data access/RBAC rules approved
- Insurance responsibilities documented

## 12. Recommendation
Treat compliance as a launch gate, not a later enhancement. No production launch should proceed until legal, payment, privacy, and fleet-operating assumptions are validated.
